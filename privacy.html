Privacy policy

Boostbook · October 2026

The short version

Boostbook doesn't collect any data. It has no account, no analytics, no advertising and no tracking, and it doesn't send anything to us unless you email us a sensor scan yourself. The only thing it ever sends to anyone else is a car's VIN, to the US NHTSA, and only when you ask it to look the VIN up online.

What stays on your iPhone

Boostbook saves these on your iPhone, in the app's own folder:

  • each car's VIN, the name you give it, and when it was last connected;
  • each car's service records and their dated backups;
  • drives you record with the Logger: where the car went (GPS), the iPhone's motion and barometer readings, and the car's values, with the car's model but not its VIN;
  • your connection settings.

They're included in your iPhone's backups like any other app data. Deleting the app deletes them.

Your car

Boostbook talks to your car only through the adapter you choose: an ENET cable, or a Wi-Fi ENET adapter on its own local network. That's why it asks for Local Network access. Talking to the car, including the live gauges, doesn't use the internet. The gauges' readings are stored only if you tap Record: then as a file on your iPhone, in Boostbook's Recordings folder, until you delete it.

Location and motion

Boostbook asks for your location and motion only for the Logger, and uses them only while you record a drive: from Record to Stop, including with the screen locked, when iOS shows that an app is using your location. The drive is saved on your iPhone and nowhere else. Drive Replay, in the web app, reads a drive you share to your computer in your browser; it doesn't upload it. The map it draws loads its imagery and terrain from Esri, OpenTopoMap, OpenStreetMap and Amazon Web Services, which see which part of the map you're looking at, as any online map's servers do.

Sharing

Data leaves Boostbook only when you export or share a file yourself, through the iOS share sheet, to wherever you choose.

Sensor scans

If you tap Scan the Car's Sensors on the Gauges tab, Boostbook asks your car's control units which values they have and makes a report: the car's model, engine and build date from its vehicle order, which control units answered and their versions, and the raw values they returned. It doesn't include the VIN. The report goes nowhere unless you tap Email the Report and send the email yourself, from your own mail app, to apps@earlish.com.au; we use it only to add support for your car.

Looking up a VIN online

If you tap Look up online on a car's details, Boostbook sends that car's VIN to the US National Highway Traffic Safety Administration's public VIN decoder (vpic.nhtsa.dot.gov) and shows what it returns. Nothing else is sent, and it only happens when you tap the button. NHTSA's own privacy policy covers what it does with requests.
